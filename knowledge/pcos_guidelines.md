# PCOS nutrition reference for personalization

This reference is context for food-scanner wording, not medical advice. PCOS diagnosis, medication, fertility care, eating-disorder risk, pregnancy, diabetes, and supplement decisions belong with a qualified clinician.

## Medical PCOS phenotypes A-D

The Rotterdam framework defines PCOS by at least two of: hyperandrogenism, ovulatory dysfunction, and polycystic ovarian morphology; the four phenotypes are combinations of those criteria. [Lizneva et al., 2016; Rotterdam criteria summary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6992448/)

- **Phenotype A, classic/full:** hyperandrogenism + ovulatory dysfunction + polycystic ovarian morphology. [Lizneva et al., 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6992448/)
- **Phenotype B, classic/non-PCOM:** hyperandrogenism + ovulatory dysfunction, without polycystic ovarian morphology. [Lizneva et al., 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6992448/)
- **Phenotype C, ovulatory:** hyperandrogenism + polycystic ovarian morphology, with ovulatory cycles. [Lizneva et al., 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6992448/)
- **Phenotype D, non-hyperandrogenic:** ovulatory dysfunction + polycystic ovarian morphology, without hyperandrogenism. [Lizneva et al., 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6992448/)

## Common online "4 types" are not formal diagnoses

Online PCOS content often labels people as **insulin-resistant**, **inflammatory**, **adrenal**, or **post-pill** PCOS. These labels can be useful for lifestyle conversation, but they are not formal diagnostic phenotypes in the Rotterdam, NIH, or AE-PCOS systems. Formal phenotypes are based on hyperandrogenism, ovulatory dysfunction, and ovarian morphology. [Lizneva et al., 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6992448/)

Loose mapping for food advice:

- **Insulin-resistant type:** can overlap with any phenotype, but is often discussed with phenotypes A or B because hyperandrogenic/anovulatory PCOS is commonly more metabolically affected in cohort studies. Treat this as a risk context, not a diagnosis. [Gupta et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29550643/)
- **Inflammatory type:** not a Rotterdam phenotype. It may describe people whose symptoms feel worse with ultra-processed foods, poor sleep, stress, or inflammatory comorbidities, but it should not override formal medical assessment. [Teede et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/108/10/2447/7242360)
- **Adrenal type:** not a Rotterdam phenotype. It usually refers to androgen excess suspected to be more adrenal than ovarian; medical evaluation matters because other adrenal disorders can mimic PCOS. [Teede et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/108/10/2447/7242360)
- **Post-pill type:** not a Rotterdam phenotype. Symptoms after stopping hormonal contraception may reveal underlying PCOS or another cycle issue; do not frame food as a cure. [Teede et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/108/10/2447/7242360)

## Core diet principles from the 2023 international PCOS guideline

- Lifestyle management is a core part of PCOS care, and healthy eating and/or physical activity are recommended for all women with PCOS to support general health, quality of life, body composition, and weight management. [Teede et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/108/10/2447/7242360)
- There is no evidence that one specific diet composition is superior for anthropometric, metabolic, hormonal, reproductive, or psychological outcomes in PCOS. [Teede et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/108/10/2447/7242360)
- Any diet pattern consistent with population healthy-eating guidance can help; advice should be sustainable, preference-aware, flexible, and not unnecessarily restrictive or nutritionally unbalanced. [Teede et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/108/10/2447/7242360)
- Healthy lifestyle can have benefits even without weight loss. [International evidence-based PCOS guideline, 2023](https://doi.org/10.26180/24003834.v1)

## Insulin-resistance dietary levers

PCOS commonly involves insulin resistance, but routine clinical insulin assays have limited usefulness; food advice should focus on practical glycemic load and diet quality rather than diagnosing insulin resistance from the app. [Teede et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/108/10/2447/7242360)

- Prefer lower-glycemic-load meals: intact grains, beans/lentils, vegetables, fruit, yogurt/kefir without added sugar, nuts/seeds, and protein-rich foods. Low-GI or low-GL approaches are plausible for PCOS because carbohydrate quality affects post-meal glucose and insulin demand, but guidelines still avoid naming a single best diet. [Barrea et al., 2023](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/15/15/3483); [Teede et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/108/10/2447/7242360)
- Aim for high fibre; general adult nutrition guidance commonly uses at least 25 g/day for women as an adequate intake target. [National Academies DRI summary](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK56068/)
- Include protein at meals and snacks where possible. Around 30% of energy from protein is a practical higher-protein pattern sometimes used for satiety and lean-mass preservation, but it is not a PCOS requirement or a prescription. [Teede et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/108/10/2447/7242360)
- Reduce added sugar and sugar-sweetened drinks because they raise glycemic load and crowd out nutrient-dense foods. [Barrea et al., 2023](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/15/15/3483)
- Omega-3-rich foods such as fatty fish, walnuts, chia, and flax can support cardiometabolic diet quality; PCOS trials of omega-3 supplementation suggest possible improvements in insulin resistance and lipids, with limitations in study size and duration. [Yang et al., 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5870911/)

## Inflammation-oriented levers

- Reduce ultra-processed foods when they are high in added sugar, refined starch, sodium, and low-quality fats; this is a diet-quality principle rather than a formal PCOS phenotype treatment. [Teede et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/108/10/2447/7242360)
- Increase polyphenol-rich whole foods: berries, leafy greens, legumes, herbs/spices, cocoa-rich dark chocolate in small portions, extra-virgin olive oil, nuts, and tea. This supports general anti-inflammatory diet quality; do not imply it treats PCOS by itself. [Cowan et al., 2023](https://bmcendocrdisord.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12902-022-01208-y)
- Include omega-3 food sources: fatty fish, walnuts, flax, chia, and other seeds. [Yang et al., 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5870911/)

## Adrenal-type considerations

For users who mention stress, cortisol, or “adrenal PCOS,” keep advice conservative: stable meal timing, adequate calories, enough carbohydrates for activity, sleep support, and magnesium-rich foods such as legumes, nuts, seeds, leafy greens, and whole grains. Under-eating can increase stress physiology and worsen cravings or binge-restrict cycles; the 2023 guideline also emphasizes emotional wellbeing, weight stigma awareness, and sustainable lifestyle support. [Teede et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/108/10/2447/7242360)

## Cravings and glucose swings

For sweet or refined-carb products, suggest pairing rather than moralizing: add protein plus fibre, such as Greek yogurt, nuts, seeds, eggs, tofu, beans, or fruit/vegetables. Protein and fibre slow digestion and generally blunt post-meal glucose excursions compared with refined carbohydrate alone; this is especially relevant when users report cravings, fatigue, or insulin-resistance concerns. [Barrea et al., 2023](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/15/15/3483)

Chromium and cinnamon are commonly discussed online for cravings or glucose. Evidence is mixed and not strong enough for the app to recommend them; small food-level cinnamon use is generally a culinary choice, not a treatment claim. [Teede et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/108/10/2447/7242360)

## Weight management

When weight loss is relevant to the user's stated goal, prefer modest, sustainable changes over aggressive restriction. The 2023 guideline supports healthy lifestyle for weight maintenance, prevention of weight gain, and/or modest weight loss, while warning against unduly restrictive or unbalanced diets. [Teede et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/108/10/2447/7242360)

A practical framing is a modest energy deficit, roughly 10-15% below maintenance when appropriate, instead of crash dieting. Preserve muscle with adequate protein and resistance training; the guideline recommends muscle-strengthening activities alongside aerobic physical activity for adults. [Teede et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/108/10/2447/7242360)

## Acne and androgen-related symptoms

For acne-prone or hyperandrogenism-focused profiles, low-glycemic eating has the strongest diet signal. A systematic review found high glycemic index/load and carbohydrate intake have a modest pro-acne effect supported by randomized trials; dairy evidence is mixed but may be pro-acnegenic in some populations, so reducing high-dairy exposure can be framed as an experiment rather than a rule. [Dall'Oglio et al., 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8971946/)

Spearmint tea has small randomized-trial support for lowering free and total testosterone over 30 days in PCOS, but objective hirsutism scores did not significantly improve in that short study. Mention only as limited evidence, not a recommendation or substitute for care. [Grant, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/ptr.2900)

## Supplements: context only

**App does not recommend supplements; this is for context only.** Supplements can interact with medications, pregnancy/fertility care, glucose-lowering drugs, and liver or kidney conditions. Do not provide dosages.

- **Inositol, including myo-inositol and D-chiro-inositol combinations such as 40:1:** RCT meta-analyses suggest possible benefits for menstrual regularity and metabolic or androgen markers, but certainty varies and guidelines do not treat it as universal standard therapy. [Greff et al., 2023](https://rbej.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12958-023-01055-z); [Showell et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30570133/)
- **Berberine:** systematic reviews suggest possible improvements in insulin resistance and some reproductive markers, but evidence quality and heterogeneity mean it should be clinician-supervised, especially with glucose-lowering drugs. [Mirzaee et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32811221/); [Ha & Song, 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39236662/)
- **Vitamin D:** meta-analyses suggest possible hormonal/metabolic effects, especially when deficiency is present, but supplementation should be based on clinical context and lab status. [Kazeminia et al., 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s43043-024-00201-w)
- **Omega-3:** PCOS RCT meta-analyses suggest possible improvements in HOMA-IR and lipid markers, but trials are generally small and short. [Yang et al., 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5870911/)

## Foods that may score poorly but can be fine in moderation

Use nuance when a product is not ideal by the rubric but is not inherently “bad.”

- **Dark chocolate:** may score lower for saturated fat, sugar, or calorie density, but small portions of cocoa-rich chocolate can fit a balanced diet and contribute polyphenols. Flag portion size and added sugar rather than saying “avoid.” [Cowan et al., 2023](https://bmcendocrdisord.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12902-022-01208-y)
- **Cheese:** may score lower for saturated fat and sodium, but it provides protein and calcium. For acne-prone users, dairy reduction can be framed as an optional trial because evidence is mixed. [Dall'Oglio et al., 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8971946/)
- **Full-fat yogurt:** may score lower for saturated fat, but plain yogurt can provide protein and fermentation benefits; added sugar matters more than fat alone for glycemic load. [Teede et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/108/10/2447/7242360)
